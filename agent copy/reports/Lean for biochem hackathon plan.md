# BPL already built this — differentiate the checker

**The verdict: the idea works, it is already published, and your opening is narrow but real.** On 7 May 2026, Bota Biosciences posted **BPL / BPL-COGEN** on bioRxiv — a Biology Protocol Language with a unit-typed grammar, a container-state engine, a three-gate deterministic compiler, diagnostic-guided repair capped at three attempts, multi-backend export, *and* a three-tier provenance/trust model — validated on **300 published *Nature Protocols* papers at 95.1 ± 8.3% fidelity with a 98.6% compile success rate inside two repair iterations** ([bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.05.05.720956](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.05.720956)). That is your pipeline, stage for stage, including the provenance labelling. It is not alone: four more 2026 systems converged on the same shape within four months — **ProtoPilot** ([arXiv:2606.31763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31763)), the **KIMM dual-agent framework** ([arXiv:2606.20120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20120)), **AEGIS** ([arXiv:2607.15620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15620)) and **ProtoAct** ([arXiv:2608.01690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01690)) — and the ancestor, **CLAIRify**, did LLM-generates-XDL-plus-rule-based-verifier-in-a-loop in March 2023 ([arXiv:2303.14100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14100)). So the architecture is correct and it is not novel. What is genuinely open, and what you should build: an **open-source** artifact (BPL's 24-page preprint contains no code- or data-availability statement and no repository; CLAIRify's repo is abandoned at 0 stars with no license), a checker whose own soundness is **machine-checked** (no proof assistant has ever been pointed at wet-lab protocols), **per-claim literature provenance bound to protocol parameters** (BPL's Declared/Calibrated/Verified tiers track *calibration state*, not which paper a number came from), and **zero LLM anywhere inside the checker** — the minority but better-defended half of the 2026 field. Target **EUCAST broth microdilution MIC for one non-fastidious organism**, because it is the only assay family whose governing standard is free, numeric, tolerance-bearing, control-mandating, *and* publishes an expected answer for a reference strain. Do not literally use Lean 4 as your checker; use a typed Python DSL and exhibit Lean as one proved theorem.

## BPL-COGEN is your architecture, published five months ago, and closed

Read the paper before you write a novelty claim. BPL's thesis is verbatim yours: biology protocols rely on "ambiguous natural-language descriptions: a medium other engineering disciplines abandoned decades ago in favor of compiler verified specification languages," with Verilog named as the model to imitate ([bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.05.05.720956](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.05.720956)). The implementation detail matters because it calibrates what a hackathon can and cannot claim. BPL's grammar is **463 lines of Lark PEG**; its type system does compile-time dimensional analysis across **~40 laboratory units** (the paper says "12 base dimensions" in Fig. 1a and "nine base physical dimensions" in the Results text — an internal inconsistency, so do not quote the number); it defines **fourteen high-level laboratory intents**; a container-state engine tracks volume, concentration, contents and capacity and rejects overflow or draw-from-empty; and layer five is the **three-tier trust model (Declared, Calibrated, Verified)** that "tracks the provenance and confidence of every state value," emitting per-step trust-transition traces for Research/GLP/GMP contexts. The compiler runs three gates — parse (grammar → typed AST), semantic (unit consistency, type safety, state coherence, identifier resolution), and plan (intent lowering to a DAG, dependency-aware scheduling, backend capability checks) — each emitting "typed diagnostics with error codes, source locations, and targeted repair hints," fed back as contextualized repair prompts with a **maximum of three attempts**. Behind that sit **1,175 compiler test cases, 14 language revision cycles, validation against 150 published protocols, and a fine-tuned 30-billion-parameter generator (BPL-Nano-30B)**. You will not match that in 48 hours and you should not pretend to.

One design detail from BPL is worth stealing outright because it is a UX decision you would otherwise get wrong: it distinguishes **errors from warnings on principle**. In the HPLC→UHPLC case study the compliance engine issued four *warnings* (plate-ratio deviation beyond the 25% pharmacopoeial equivalence limit, packing-type change, pore-size change, 5.1× backpressure increase) rather than errors, "by design: errors indicate physically invalid operations; warnings flag methodological changes that alter the analytical basis and require informed human judgement," with the analyst's acceptance of each recorded in the audit trail ([bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.05.05.720956](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.05.720956)). That line is the correct answer to the judge who asks "what if the checker is wrong about science?"

The surrounding cluster tells you where the field splits, and the split is your positioning axis. **BPL-COGEN and KIMM use deterministic checkers** — KIMM's parser agent formalizes natural language and then "a rule-based mapping engine deterministically incorporates the robot platform's operational constraints," with a heterogeneous LLM only in the validation role ([arXiv:2606.20120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20120)). **AEGIS and ProtoAct put a model inside the checker** — AEGIS "pairs a curated machine-readable assay rule database with an LLM that reasons over OT-2 Python code," reaching adjusted F1 0.97 on assay-invariant violations like tip reuse between a PCR template and a no-template control ([arXiv:2607.15620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15620)); ProtoAct's RefineChecker is a model ([arXiv:2608.01690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01690)). Being strictly deterministic aligns you with the stronger half and lets you honestly critique the other half. The strongest available citation for *why* is the verification-gap survey: of 24 runnable AI-scientist systems, **83% release code but only 38% release the seeds or traces needed to reproduce a run and only 38% report any novelty-verification method**, and among nine closed-loop systems "**no LLM-era system in the corpus demonstrates an externally validated in-loop oracle**" ([arXiv:2608.05179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05179)) — the authors themselves caution these rates are "directional audit evidence," so quote them as such. Pair it with the benchmark evidence that LLMs fail *precisely* where a deterministic checker bites: BioProBench (ICML 2026) finds "systemic weaknesses in handling causal logic, quantitative precision, and structured generation," with step-ordering exact-match around 50% and generation BLEU under 15% ([arXiv:2505.07889](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07889)), and ProtoPilot measures **OpentronsAI — a shipping commercial LLM protocol generator — at a 32.35% pass rate against its own 88.24%** ([arXiv:2606.31763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31763)).

Commercially, the state of the art is **simulation, not verification**. Opentrons shipped Protocol Visualization for the Flex in March 2026, explicitly framed as "an inspection layer … between AI-generated experimental plans and robotic execution" ([Opentrons](https://opentrons.com/archives/news/opentrons-viz-ai-lab-workflows)). Simulation catches collisions, missing labware and volume accounting; it does not know what a control condition is, whether a dilution series has the right fold factor, or whether a number has a citation. AEGIS's entire premise — protocols that are "syntactically valid but violate assay-specific invariants" — is the proof that gap is real. That is your cleanest honest gap statement.

|---|---|

## Do not make Lean 4 the checker; prove one theorem and rename the pitch

**Decision: a Pydantic v2 + pint typed Python DSL is the checker. Lean 4 is one Mathlib-free file proving one theorem, exhibited live, owned by one person, and never on the critical path.** Three independent lines of evidence force this.

First, the Lean units ecosystem is three small research artifacts, not a library. ATOMSLab's `LeanDimensionalAnalysis` formalizes dimensions as maps from base dimensions to exponents in a commutative ring, proves they form an Abelian group under multiplication, covers SI base units and the Buckingham Pi theorem, and discharges homogeneity via typeclass inference plus a custom `evalAutoDim` tactic ([arXiv:2509.13142](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13142v1)) — but the repo is **13 commits, 5 stars, Apache-2.0**, depends on Mathlib (pinned around 4.23.0-rc1), and the authors state that epsilon-operator definitions are `noncomputable`, which **prevents direct evaluation via `#eval`** ([GitHub ATOMSLab/LeanDimensionalAnalysis](https://github.com/ATOMSLab/LeanDimensionalAnalysis)). `ericeallen/lambda-s` is a full mechanization of a units-of-measure calculus — statics, dynamics, denotational semantics, Pi theorem — Apache-2.0 and actively committed, but **single-author with 0 stars** ([GitHub ericeallen/lambda-s](https://github.com/ericeallen/lambda-s)); the two notes differ trivially on its last-push date (one says 13 Sep 2026, one says 1 Oct 2026), which only reinforces that it is a moving target. **Mathlib itself has no units-of-measure module** — the 2025 dimensional-analysis paper's own framing is that it contributes dimensions *because* Mathlib already supplies the analysis but not the dimensions, and the existence of three separate standalone projects is the corroborating evidence. SciLean is the wrong dependency: no units support was found in its core.

Second — and this is the decisive argument — there is a published finding that dependently typed compiler feedback is a *bad* signal for an LLM repair loop: "compiler-level feedback is identified as the critical bottleneck in dependently typed languages compared to execution-based feedback" ([arXiv:2602.11481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11481), on Idris). The converse also holds: "Rust compiler error messages are often helpful for LLMs to fix mistakes" ([arXiv:2512.02567](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02567v1)). Your repair loop's quality is bounded by message quality, and raw Lean errors are the worst-shaped messages available. If Lean is in the pipeline at all, **every expected Lean failure must be mapped to a hand-written `Finding` with a stable code**, never piped to the model raw. (Both of these are code-repair studies; no study exists on repair loops over scientific-protocol artifacts, so the transfer is an inference.)

Third, the Lean precedent that does exist points at the wrong half of the problem. **LeanBET** formalizes BET surface-area calculation in Lean 4 — window enumeration soundness and completeness, monotonicity, linear regression agreeing with its specification-level definition, floating-point correctness tied to real-number proofs — matching the BETSI Python reference to machine precision on 18 of 19 isotherms ([arXiv:2605.16169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16169)). That is the template for "take a lab-relevant *computation*, specify it in Lean, prove the implementation matches." It is data analysis, not protocol execution. The protocol side is open, which is exactly why a *tiny, honest* Lean artifact is high-value demo content.

The shape that actually ships, if one person owns it end to end: a **Mathlib-free** Lean 4 package with `structure Vol where µL : Rat` and `structure Conc where molPerL : Rat` as *distinct nominal types* (unit safety with zero dependent-type machinery and zero Mathlib build cost); one real lemma about dilution/volume conservation stated over `Rat` so it is decidable and computable; and range predicates as `Bool`-valued functions with `example : inRange 37 tempRange = true := by decide`. `decide` is cheap here because the propositions are closed and the arithmetic is over `Rat`. The deliverable is a Lean file that **exports a checked protocol fragment and fails to compile when the fragment is wrong, shown live**. `native_decide` is the escape hatch but adds a trust axiom; you will not need it. Choosing `Fraction`/`Rat` over `float` is the single most important representation decision in the whole build regardless of language — it makes C1V1 = C2V2 an *exact* equality, removes epsilon bikeshedding, and is what makes `decide` viable. pint has a documented history of `DimensionalityError` from floating-point noise in fractional unit exponents ([pint changelog](https://pint.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changes.html)), which is independent reason to use pint for dimensionality bookkeeping only and do all comparisons on exact rationals.

**Honest framing for the name.** Do not say "Lean for biochem" on stage as a claim about mechanism. Say: *"We built what Verilog's toolchain is to hardware, for one class of microbiology assay: a typed protocol language, a deterministic compiler, and a repair loop. The compiler is Python. One safety property — the dilution algebra — is additionally proved in Lean 4, and the Lean file refuses to compile when we corrupt the fragment. Lean checks that one theorem; Python enforces the other ninety per cent of the rules."* That sentence is true, it is still impressive, and it survives the question "so is Lean actually verifying your protocol?" A project that claims Lean verifies the protocol loses the technicality points it was fishing for the moment a judge asks what Mathlib pin it uses.

## Scope it to EUCAST broth microdilution: the only free standard with numbers, controls, *and* an expected answer

**Primary scope: EUCAST/ISO 20776-1 broth microdilution MIC determination for one non-fastidious aerobic species, on one 96-well plate, comparing two approved antibiotics, with the matching EUCAST QC reference strain as a built-in positive control.** This is literally the textbook instantiation of the team's own motivating prompt, so there is no scope-fitting tax. The in-family stretch is the **checkerboard / FIC index** — same medium, same inoculum, same incubation, same readout, rearranged as a 2-D grid, plus one published arithmetic index. The second, deliberately thin constraint pack that evidences generalization is a **BCA/Bradford standard curve** (Pierce BCA's published linear working range of **20–2000 µg/mL** for BSA under the standard protocol, 37 °C for 30 min, read at 562 nm, with a mandatory per-assay standard curve — [Pierce BCA user guide MAN0011430](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0011430_Pierce_BCA_Protein_Asy_UG.pdf)).

The discriminating property is not "does a standard exist" but "does the standard publish **numeric values with tolerances**, **mandatory control conditions**, *and* **an expected answer for a reference material**." Only broth microdilution has all three for free. qPCR has MIQE, but MIQE prescribes *what you must report*, not numeric windows, so the checker degrades into a completeness checklist ([MIQE 2009](https://www.gene-quantification.de/miqe-bustin-et-al-clin-chem-2009.pdf)). ELISA's parameters are defined per kit insert, so "citation for every parameter" collapses to "citation = this vendor's PDF." Buffer prep is the easiest to check arithmetically and the least impressive, with no governing standard and no required controls. MTT/XTT has ISO 10993-5's ≥70% viability criterion but the standard is paywalled and the number is an interpretation threshold, not a parameter set.

Three attribution caveats to put on the slide rather than bury. **ISO 20776-1:2019 is paywalled** and you never need it: EUCAST states its own recommendations are "in complete agreement" with ISO and restates the operative numbers. The free EUCAST QC tables footnote their MIC ranges as derived from **CLSI M100-S35 (2025)** except those in bold/italics, all EUCAST-validated — so you can honestly say the checker encodes internationally harmonised QC ranges without touching a paywalled document. And the combination layer has **no standards body behind it at all**: neither EUCAST nor ISO publishes a standardised bacterial checkerboard method, so the FICI layer cites an editorial and derived analyses. Over-claiming on FICI is the single biggest credibility risk in this scope.

Why these particular numbers make a *great demo* rather than merely a correct one. The viable-count instruction is a **self-consistency theorem the checker can re-derive**: 10 µL into 10 mL is 1:1001 ≈ 1:1000; 5×10⁵ CFU/mL × 10⁻³ × 0.1 mL = 50 CFU, the exact centre of the published 20–80 window. The published standard hands you both the procedure and the expected numeric outcome, which is unusual. And the 96-well plate is **exactly saturated** by ten dilutions plus two mandated controls, so adding an eleventh dilution step necessarily destroys a required control well — a capacity violation that arises from the real published method rather than from a contrived example. The checkerboard makes this sharper: an 8×8 two-agent grid consumes **64 of 96 wells**, leaving 32 that must still hold both single-agent series plus growth and sterility controls. FICI itself is pure arithmetic over values the protocol already declares — `FICI = MIC_A,combo/MIC_A,alone + MIC_B,combo/MIC_B,alone` — so the checker can verify that both single-agent MIC wells exist (the index is undefined without them, a very common real design error), that the grid brackets each agent's expected MIC range rather than running off-scale, and that the reported classification matches the computed index. Best of all, the cut-offs' published justification is *itself a precision argument* — "because of the twofold drug dilution scheme and the 1-dilution error of single-drug susceptibility testing methods … cutoffs of 0.5 and 4 were suggested" ([PMC2812160](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2812160/)) — which licenses a genuinely novel-feeling check: a protocol claiming to distinguish FICI 0.45 from 0.55 off a two-fold dilution series is not supported by the method's resolution.

For grounding data, two APIs were verified live on 1 October 2026 and both are free with no key. PubChem PUG-REST returned ciprofloxacin as **CID 2764, MW 331.34, XLogP −1.1**; ChEMBL REST returned CHEMBL8 with ATC classifications including **J01MA02** ([PubChem PUG-REST docs](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/docs/pug-rest); [ChEMBL API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/docs)). MW is load-bearing: "2 mg/L" versus "6 µM" of ciprofloxacin is only reconcilable through 331.34, and getting that from an API with a URL per value *is* the citation-per-parameter story. `mic.eucast.org` holds **more than 40,000 MIC distributions** with ECOFFs, free on the web but with **no documented REST API** — plan Excel downloads or scraping, not an API call ([mic.eucast.org](https://mic.eucast.org/)). Skip DrugBank: it is license-gated and nothing in the demo needs it.

## Architecture: immutable design block, mutable execution block, zero LLM in the checker

The pipeline, concretely. **Stage 1** is a planner agent producing a natural-language experiment plan from the user request. **Stage 2** is a compiler agent emitting your IR as JSON, constrained at generation time by the JSON Schema that Pydantic v2 exports for free — so "unit is nonsense" is a cheap schema error rather than an expensive semantic one. **Stage 3** is the deterministic checker: a plain Python rule registry of decorator-registered pure functions `(protocol) -> Iterable[Finding]`, no engine, no model. **Stage 4** is the repair loop, with findings returned as a batch. **Stage 5** exports a human step-sheet plus an Opentrons Python protocol plus the Lean fragment plus a signed findings report.

**The IR.** Every numeric parameter is `Quantity{value: Fraction, unit: UCUM-string, source: Ref}`. Parse `value` as `Fraction`/`Decimal`, never `float`. Validate `unit` against UCUM via **`ucumvert`**, which parses the full UCUM grammar and constructs pint units from UCUM strings (release 0.2.0 supports UCUM v2.2, June 2024 — [GitHub dalito/ucumvert](https://github.com/dalito/ucumvert)). Convert to pint once in a Pydantic `field_validator`, and treat pint as a unit *system*, not as your arithmetic. Avoid pint's known sharp edges: `auto_reduce_dimensions=True` triggers spurious `DimensionalityError` ([issue #902](https://github.com/hgrecco/pint/issues/902)), and subclassing `Quantity` breaks `__setitem__` silently ([issue #826](https://github.com/hgrecco/pint/issues/826)).

**Split the IR into a `design` block and an `execution` block. This is the anti-degradation design and it is the project's real differentiator.** `design` holds replicate counts per group, the required control set, the declared endpoints/readouts and the declared sample size. `execution` holds the steps. **The repair prompt is only ever allowed to return a new `execution` block; the orchestrator re-attaches `design` from the *original* protocol, not from the model's output.** The LLM cannot delete a control condition it is not permitted to rewrite. Then add a `DESIGN*` rule family that checks `execution` against `design`, so dropping a control now *creates* an error instead of removing one: `DESIGN001` control declared but absent from execution; `DESIGN002` replicate count below `design.required_replicates`; `DESIGN003` declared readout never produced. Run a **diff-based monotonicity gate before the checker**, rejecting any candidate where step count dropped, a step with `role ∈ {positive_control, negative_control, blank}` disappeared, a replicate count decreased, or a declared readout vanished — reported as `GUARD*` findings so the model learns the boundary. The asymmetry is what makes the strict gate safe: a legitimate repair almost never needs to delete a control. Finally, since every numeric parameter carries a `source`, make it an error to *change* a cited value while keeping the citation (`CITE002_VALUE_DIVERGES_FROM_SOURCE`) — that blocks the laziest degradation path of all, silently retuning a literature-derived concentration until the arithmetic closes. **No published work exists on this specific failure mode (an LLM satisfying a validator by weakening the experiment), so present this as an untested design, not a known technique — and note that is precisely what makes it the most interesting thing you could claim.**

**Error-code design.** Codes must be **stable and never reused**, because they are what lets you detect oscillation and build a per-code fix-hint table. Namespace by family: `UNIT*` (dimensional consistency), `ARITH*` (C1V1=C2V2, fold factors), `VOL*` (capacity, both bounds), `RANGE*` (equipment and standard ranges), `DF*` (dataflow: `DF001_UNDEFINED_REAGENT`), `DESIGN*`, `GUARD*`, `CITE*`, `REACT*` (reactivity, including fail-closed `REACT_UNKNOWN_GROUP` at severity=warning), `SIM*` (`SIM_OPENTRONS`). Each `Finding` is a frozen dataclass serialized as:

```json
{ "code": "VOL002", "severity": "error",
  "loc": "/steps/4/transfers/0/volume",
  "message": "well A12 receives 350 uL, labware max is 300 uL",
  "observed": "350 uL", "limit": "300 uL",
  "evidence": "https://labware.opentrons.com/... (totalLiquidVolume)",
  "fix_hint": "reduce transfer to <= 300 uL, or split across A12 and B12",
  "invariant_guard": ["replicate_count", "control_set"] }
```

`loc` must be a **JSON Pointer into the IR**, not a line number — the model edits a JSON document, and Pydantic v2 validation errors already carry a `loc` tuple, so native and custom findings share one location convention for free. `observed` and `limit` are separate from `message` because the prose is for humans while the structured pair is what lets the model compute a correct new value instead of guessing. `evidence` (a URL) is the **anti-hallucination field**: it shows a human that the limit came from a labware definition or a EUCAST table, not from the checker's opinion.

**The checks, in implementation order.** Dimensional consistency via pint. C1V1 = C2V2 as **exact rational equality** (`c1*v1 == c2*v2` over `Fraction`, no tolerance; if a tolerance is unavoidable make it a named logged policy like `REL_TOL = Fraction(1,1000)` and put it in the error message so the model knows its slack). Serial dilution: compute `C_{i+1} = C_i · v_transfer/(v_transfer + v_diluent)` recursively over rationals and compare to the protocol's **declared** value — check the declaration, not just the computation, because the model's stated intent ("10-fold serial dilution") is the invariant you are protecting and a silent recompute lets it "fix" errors by changing its intent. Volume accounting as a forward abstract-interpretation pass: `balance: dict[(labware_id, well_id), Fraction]`, folding dispense/aspirate over steps in order, asserting `0 ≤ balance[w] ≤ capacity(w)` **after every step** so the error can name the step index; the negative bound catches aspirating from an empty well, a different bug class. Define-before-use as reaching definitions: seed `defined` with the declared manifest, and for each step in topological order flag every `uses` identifier not in `defined`, then add its `produces`. Controls, replicates and ordering as **relational existence and counting queries** whose required values come from the `design` block, not from the checker's imagination. And source-reference completeness as a generic walk over `model_fields`: for every field typed as `Quantity`, assert `source is not None` and resolves into the reference list — about fifteen lines, and the highest ratio of demo credibility to implementation cost in the whole project.

**Capacity data.** Vendor the Opentrons labware definitions rather than calling Opentrons. Copy the standard JSON from `shared-data/labware/definitions/2/` and load by `loadName`; read `wells[*].totalLiquidVolume` (a positive number, **in microliters**) for capacity, `ordering` for column-major well order (which is what serial-dilution-across-a-row checks need), and `parameters` for `isTiprack` ([labware schema 2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Opentrons/opentrons/edge/shared-data/labware/schemas/2.json)). The API-side binding is confirmed: "the maximum volume property in the well definition is set by … the `totalLiquidVolume` property" ([Opentrons Wells and Liquids](https://docs.opentrons.com/python-api/reference/wells-liquids/)). Check `shared-data`'s LICENSE before copying files in — that was not verified.

**Second oracle.** Emit an Opentrons Python protocol and call `simulate.simulate(protocol_file, custom_labware_paths=[...])` **in process**; it "either returns (if the simulation has no problems) or raises an exception" ([Opentrons execute-simulate reference](https://docs.opentrons.com/python-api/reference/execute-simulate/)), a trivially wrappable contract. Report any exception as one `SIM_OPENTRONS` finding. The value is not that it checks more than you, but that it checks *differently*, and a clean run is evidence your IR is physically realisable. Be honest about what it covers: pipette max volume ([#5815](https://github.com/Opentrons/opentrons/issues/5815)), dispense-exceeds-aspirate, tip presence and tracking ([release notes](https://github.com/Opentrons/opentrons/blob/edge/api/release-notes.md)), and API-version compatibility. Borrow the microfluidics field's vocabulary for the two layers — your checker is **pre-synthesis** verification, the simulator is **post-synthesis** ([arXiv:2211.04719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04719)) — it is a cheap credibility win and it is accurate.

**Provenance, three tiers, each with a different guarantee.** `status: "cited"` is checkable deterministically: DOI regex → `doi.org` HEAD or Crossref `/works/{doi}` → fuzzy title and first-author-surname match against registered metadata → **Retraction Watch** lookup. That last one is the highest-value-per-line check in the project: Crossref acquired and now distributes Retraction Watch under **CC-BY 4.0** as a ~50,000-row dataset via a public git repo, and surfaces it inline in `/works/` ([Crossref](https://www.crossref.org/blog/news-crossref-and-retraction-watch/)) — load the CSV into memory and a cited-but-retracted paper becomes a hard, deterministic, demo-able failure with no API call at inference time. Mind the limits, **effective 1 December 2025**: public pool 5 req/s for single records but **1 req/s for list/query endpoints**; polite pool (just add `mailto=`) 10 req/s single DOI and 3 req/s lists ([Crossref](https://www.crossref.org/blog/announcing-changes-to-rest-api-rate-limits/)). Resolve by DOI wherever possible. Cross-check retraction against PubMed's `PublicationType` field (3 req/s without a key, 10 with). `status: "derived"` is checkable by **re-execution**: the checker takes `formula_id` from a *named, whitelisted* registry, substitutes referenced inputs, evaluates with units, and compares. **Reject free-text formulas** — a free-text formula field is just another place to hallucinate. `status: "speculative"` is checkable only negatively (no source, no derivation, visibly marked in the rendered output); its value is forcing honesty about coverage. Borrow vocabulary rather than adopting ontologies: `prov:wasDerivedFrom`, `prov:Activity`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy` from PROV-O (51 classes, a W3C Rec) and ECO codes as a controlled vocabulary for `evidence_type`, citing SEPIO (210 object properties) and ECO (3,337 classes) as alignment targets you deliberately did not instantiate ([survey, PMC12376154](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12376154/)). The survey's own conclusion — that "assertion and evidence ontologies lack such standardization" unlike PROV-O — is your justification. If you use the Anthropic Citations API for quote extraction, note the deterministic gate it buys: `cited_text` is **extracted from the document rather than generated**, with character ranges, so `source_document[start:end] == cited_text` is a model-free assertion costing one string comparison ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/introducing-citations-api)).

**Hazard data.** PubChem PUG-View only, for MVP: one function `ghs(cid) -> {pictograms, signal_word, hazard_statements[]}` via the "GHS Classification" heading, **no authentication**, cached to a local JSON file on first fetch ([PUG-View docs](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/docs/pug-view)). The documented request policy is **no more than five requests per second** ([IUPAC FAIR Chemistry Cookbook](https://iupac.github.io/WFChemCookbook/datasources/pubchem_pugrest1.html), citing PubChem's own volume-limitation page). **Pre-warming that cache is the single highest-value twenty minutes of ops work in the project**, because a live API dependency is the most likely thing to break on stage. Reactivity is the honest weak point and is handled below.

**Repair-loop control.** Model self-correction has been formalized as a two-state Markov chain parameterized by **Error Introduction Rate and Error Correction Rate**, where low rates (typical of strong models) converge slowly and high rates converge fast to a poor steady state ([arXiv:2604.22273](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2) — read at summary level; do not quote specific threshold numbers). So: **hard cap at 3–5 rounds**, then fail and surface findings to a human rather than shipping. **Require monotone progress on the lexicographic key `(n_errors, n_warnings)`** and abort if round *n+1* is not strictly better. **Detect oscillation** by hashing the sorted `(code, loc)` multiset each round; a repeat means the loop is cycling. **Keep the full history in context** — prior protocol, prior findings, what changed — because error-aware repair loops that retain the erroneous artifact, the error messages and the references outperform stateless retries ([arXiv:2601.00509](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00509)). And **send all findings at once**, because repairing one at a time invites changes that break a rule the model cannot see. Multi-oracle normalization (Python registry + Opentrons simulate + Lean export, all emitting the same `Finding` schema) follows the same multi-tool pattern.

**Stretch only, in this order:** clingo for the relational layer — but do **not** use unsat cores. `SolveHandle.core()` returns solver literals "whose signs do not map naively onto assumption symbols" and the core is documented as **not minimal** ([clingo docs](https://potassco.org/clingo/python-api/5.7/clingo/solving.html)). Instead invert the formulation so violations are *derived atoms* (`violation("CTRL001", G, "missing negative control") :- assay_group(G), not step(_, G, negative_control).`) and enumerate `violation/3` from the single answer set. Precise per-violation diagnostics, no core machinery, no clingo-explaid. Skip Z3 entirely for MVP: checking a *fully concrete* protocol is evaluation, not solving; Z3 earns its place only for repair *synthesis* or parameterised templates. Explicitly out of scope for 48 hours: adopting LabOP (dormant since **2024-10-07**, its spec repo since 2023-01-16) or Autoprotocol (dormant since 2023) or XDL (no formal 2.0 schema) as the IR; OWL+SHACL; LinkML; Soufflé; OPA/Rego; vendoring ATOMSLab's Lean library; ECHA or NIOSH scraping; SiLA 2.

The mutation suite is what converts "we built a checker" into "we measured a checker." ## Make Modal load-bearing and Devin verifiable, not decorative

**Modal.** A single `@app.function` wrapping a frontier-model API call reads as token use; judges on that track have seen dozens. The defensible story is that **your checker is deterministic, CPU-only, pure and fast, so it maps perfectly** — and that the heavy formal toolchain lives in a prebuilt Image, so a multi-minute install happens once at build time and is free on every invocation. Modal itself leads with "scale out batch jobs to run massively in parallel" and documents `Function.map()` as the way to parallelize "the same function repeatedly with different independent inputs" ([Modal scale-out guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/scale)). "Watch 2,000 protocols validate in twenty seconds" is a claim judges can verify live, and it doubles as your eval harness, so one implementation buys two story beats.

Use the **post-1.0 names only**, because several decorators were renamed in the 1.0 cycle and old names emit deprecation warnings mid-demo ([Modal 1.0 migration guide](https://modal.com/docs/guide/modal-1-0-migration)): `@modal.web_endpoint` → **`@modal.fastapi_endpoint`**; `allow_concurrent_inputs=N` → **`@modal.concurrent(max_inputs=N)`**; `keep_warm` / `concurrency_limit` / `container_idle_timeout` → **`min_containers` / `max_containers` / `scaledown_window`**; `modal.Mount` and `mount=` → **`Image.add_local_dir` / `add_local_file`**; `Image.copy_local_dir` → `add_local_dir`; implicit automounting → explicit **`Image.add_local_python_source`** (the single most likely cause of "works locally, `ModuleNotFoundError` remotely"); `.lookup()` → **`.from_name()`**; `@modal.build` → `modal.Volume` or `Image.run_function`; and `modal.gpu.H100()` → the plain string **`"H100"`**. `return_exceptions=True` on `.map()` is effectively mandatory: a fan-out of thousands of generated protocols will have individual failures, and the default aborts the entire map.

Budget realistically. **Starter is $30/month of credits with a hard cap of 100 containers and 10 GPU concurrency** ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)); the Function-level platform limit is 4,000 containers, but the plan cap bites first. So say "3,000 protocols across 100 parallel workers in N seconds," not "3,000 simultaneous containers." Cost is a non-issue on the CPU side — at $0.0000131 per core-second, 3,000 checker runs at two seconds each is roughly **eight cents** — and a T4 at $0.000164/s is about **59 cents an hour**, so one small open-weights entailment model served for the whole event sits comfortably inside the free tier. What *will* exhaust it is `min_containers` on a GPU function, so pick **one** GPU role. For demo determinism: `modal deploy` (not `modal serve`) the night before, set `min_containers=1` on the endpoint and the checker, widen `scaledown_window` from its 60-second default, and verify the Image hash is unchanged so no rebuild fires — redeploying with unchanged config is a documented no-op ([managing deployments](https://modal.com/docs/guide/managing-deployments)). Note that **rollback is a Team/Enterprise feature**, so your safety net on Starter is a known-good git tag. Secondary uses that are cheap and genuine: `modal.Sandbox` for LLM-generated protocol code, with `block_network=True` plus `outbound_domain_allowlist` plus a short timeout as a concrete, demoable safety argument ([Sandbox networking](https://modal.com/docs/guide/sandbox-networking)); a `modal.Queue` of candidate protocols plus a `modal.Dict` as shared stop/best-so-far state, mirroring Modal's own documented crawler example; and `@modal.asgi_app()` for the demo UI so there is a public URL on the slide.

**The one real risk: no published Modal example installs a Lean 4 toolchain.** The mechanism is clearly supported (`apt_install` + `run_commands` + `env` to set PATH, with elan as the installer), and Modal has documented precedents for heavy non-Python binaries — Blender, Datasette, and the ESMFold2 / Chai-1 / Boltz-2 protein-folding stacks ([Modal examples](https://modal.com/docs/examples)) — but nobody has published the exact recipe and no Mathlib-on-Modal build timing exists. Put the Lean build cache in a **Volume** (`version=2`, because v1 performs best under 50,000 files and a Mathlib tree is large) and treat this as the riskiest build step. Going Mathlib-free, as recommended above, is also what makes this risk survivable.

**Devin.** The challenge is "pick a published paper, reproduce a key result, push past it," and the whole game is manufacturing a **machine-checkable success criterion before the session starts**. Cognition's own rule is the **three-hour rule** — "if a task would take you three hours or less, Devin can most likely do it" — with the binding requirement being verifiable outcomes, sufficient context, and explicit end states; tasks with "ambiguous endpoints" are documented poor fits ([When to Use Devin](https://docs.devin.ai/essential-guidelines/when-to-use-devin)). "Get this paper working" has no criterion. "Produce `repro/check.py` that asserts the reported metric is within ±X of the paper's Table 2 value and exits 0" does. That reframing is the whole ballgame.

Reproduction-target ranking, easiest first:

1. **Wong et al. 2024, *Nature* 626:177–185 — best single target.** Explainable deep learning over chemical space; the identified structural class was selective against MRSA and VRE. **Chemprop checkpoints for antibiotic activity, cytotoxicity and proton-motive-force alteration are deposited on Zenodo**, code is on GitHub, and there is a companion *Nature Protocols* paper making the pipeline step-by-step reproducible ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06887-8); [Zenodo 10095879](https://zenodo.org/records/10095879); [github.com/felixjwong/antibioticsai](https://github.com/felixjwong/antibioticsai); [Nature Protocols](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-024-01084-x)). Reproduce: load the checkpoints, regenerate the released predictions, check they match the deposited values. Hours, mostly environment setup. **Push past it:** feed the top-ranked hits into your generator, emit a EUCAST-compliant microdilution plate plus a checkerboard against a comparator, and have the checker certify it — "the ML paper ends at a ranked list; we emit the next experiment, machine-checked against EUCAST."
2. **Stokes et al. 2020, *Cell* 180:688–702 — equally fast, more famous.** Run the public Chemprop model over the Drug Repurposing Hub and recover halicin's high rank; the public web predictor makes a live demo possible in minutes ([Cell PDF](https://www.cell.com/cell/pdf/S0092-8674(20)30102-1.pdf); [github.com/swansonk14/chemprop](https://github.com/swansonk14/chemprop)). Same extension.
3. **Brochado 2018 (*Nature* 559:259–263) / Cacace 2023 (*Nature Microbiology* 8:2196–2212) — best validation corpus, risky as recomputation.** Nearly 3,000 and ~8,000 dose-resolved combinations respectively, browsable live at [apps.embl.de/combact](https://apps.embl.de/combact/). Convert published interaction calls into machine-checkable checkerboard designs and report how many you can propose a valid confirmatory plate for. **Flag the trap: these papers use their own Bliss/Loewe-style interaction scores, not FICI**, so "reproduce the metric" is not a one-liner. Neither data-availability statement was read, so do not assume the raw matrices download cleanly.
4. **BioProBench / ProtocolQA — most defensible "we measured it" claim.** Reproduce the weak published baselines on a subset, then show the static checker as a verifier/repair loop improves error-correction and generation scores on the AST subset. This also supports the "it generalizes" story because the corpus spans thousands of protocols from many assay families.

Operationally: commit the paper PDF **in the repo** alongside a hand-written `CLAIM.md` naming the single number, its location, and the agreed tolerance. Encode the repeatable procedure as a **Playbook** with the documented six sections — Overview, Procedure (imperative, MECE, one action per line), Specifications (postconditions and deliverable format), Advice and Pointers, **Forbidden Actions**, What's Needed From User ([Creating Playbooks](https://docs.devin.ai/product-guides/creating-playbooks)). The Forbidden Actions section is where the hours are saved: *do not retrain from scratch; do not re-download datasets, use `data/`; do not modify `vendor/`; do not substitute a different metric;* and the one you must not omit — ***do not change the published value in `CLAIM.md` to match your output***, which is the reproduction-specific analogue of reward hacking. Put durable conventions in a committed **`.agents/skills/<name>/SKILL.md`** with `name`, `description`, `allowed-tools` frontmatter, because **Knowledge is a deprecated feature being sunset in favour of Skills** ([Skills guide](https://docs.devin.ai/product-guides/skills); [Knowledge guide](https://docs.devin.ai/product-guides/knowledge)) — following an older tutorial here is the most likely way to waste an hour. Then follow Cognition's own advice to "**run 2+ Devins in parallel with the same playbook to quickly identify possible errors**," which here doubles as independent replication, and start a third session building the extension harness against the `check.py` exit-code contract before the first two finish. Checkpoint every 20–30 minutes, because independent reviewers' most consistent complaint is that Devin "never tells you when it's out of its depth" (from secondary aggregator reviews — [idlen.io](https://www.idlen.io/blog/devin-ai-engineer-review-limits-2026/) — whose SWE-bench 13.86% figure dates from the 2024 evaluation era and is almost certainly stale).

Set expectations with the literature, because it is a gift rather than a liability. **PaperBench's best in-paper agent scored 21.0%** replicating 20 ICML 2024 papers from scratch against hierarchical author-co-developed rubrics decomposed into 8,316 gradable tasks ([arXiv:2504.01848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848)); a secondary summary reports o1 at 24.4% against a **human ML PhD baseline of 41.4% over 48 hours** ([blog.pebblous.ai](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/paper-code-reproducibility/en/) — aggregator, verify before quoting). **CORE-Bench provides code *and* data and the best agent still reached only 21% on its hardest tier**, implicating environment configuration and dependency management as the dominant failure mode ([arXiv:2409.11363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11363)). **ResearchCodeBench, which asks for implementation of a described contribution rather than full replication, tops out at 37.3%** ([arXiv:2506.02314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02314)). That gradient is your target-selection rule: pick a paper whose code and data you already have, and reproduce **one number, not a system**. The joint story that wins both tracks at once — and that degrades gracefully if either half breaks — is: **Devin reproduces the paper and emits a verifiable harness; Modal runs that harness at a scale the paper never did.** The fan-out is only possible because of Modal, which makes it load-bearing; and the harness alone is a deliverable, as is the fan-out alone.

## What to claim, what to refuse to claim, and the limits that will be probed

Claim, in roughly this order: that you implemented the emerging 2026 consensus architecture **openly**, where the field's closest system is closed and its ancestor is abandoned; that the checker contains **zero LLM calls**, which you can demonstrate by grepping the module; that **every numeric parameter carries a citation and the checker enforces it**, with a status histogram showing what fraction is cited, derived or speculative; that a `design` block the repair loop **cannot edit** structurally prevents the model from satisfying the checker by weakening the experiment; that one safety property is **proved in Lean 4** and the proof file refuses to compile on a corrupted fragment; that the generated protocol **also passes Opentrons' own simulator**; and that you measured all of this against a mutation suite with a published precision/recall table rather than asserting it.

Refuse to claim, out loud, on a dedicated slide. **A deterministic checker cannot validate scientific merit.** It cannot tell you whether the experiment answers the question, whether the controls are the *right* controls as opposed to merely present, or whether the replicate count is adequately *powered* — that last one requires an effect-size assumption the checker does not have. It verifies that a citation **exists** and is **unchanged**, not that it is **apt**. **CAMEO Chemicals has no API and no documented bulk download**: the compatibility matrix is generated inside the tool, and the reactive-groups browse page exposes no export ([NOAA](https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/oil-and-chemical-spills/chemical-spills/chemical-reactivity-worksheet); [reactive groups](https://cameochemicals.noaa.gov/browse/react)). So your reactivity check is a **hand-curated table of 10–20 pairs covering the reagents your demo actually uses**, each row carrying its `/react/{id}` citation, presented as a seed table with a documented extraction path — never as CAMEO coverage. Assigning an arbitrary reagent to a reactive group is itself a chemistry judgment, so **fail closed** with a distinct `REACT_UNKNOWN_GROUP` warning rather than silently passing; fail-closed-with-a-distinct-code is what keeps the checker honest about its own coverage. **Scientific-domain entailment models perform near chance**: MiniCheck-7B sits around 50 balanced accuracy on SciFact, and while MiniCheck-FT5 at 770M params reaches 74.7% BAcc on general-domain LLM-AggreFact versus GPT-4's 75.3% at **400× lower cost ($0.24 vs $107)** ([MiniCheck, EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.499.pdf)), that general-domain strength does not transfer — so do not stake the demo on an entailment model's semantic verdict about biochemistry. (The specific 64.7-BAcc ThinknCheck comparison figure comes from a search summary whose primary source was not located; the *direction* is well-attested, the number is not.) Related and equally important: measured unsupported-citation rates for agentic scientific synthesis **range from ~3% to ~18% depending only on the verifier's strictness**, and negative-specific agreement *between* verifiers is only **0.27–0.30** ([arXiv:2607.20527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20527)). A single headline number is therefore not a result; **publish the verifier and the threshold**, and consider that paper's model-free BM25 matcher (recall 0.94 on the supported class) plus a split-conformal layer as the cheapest way to say "≤X% of unflagged citations are unsupported, at 90% confidence." Finally, three narrower limits: whether Opentrons simulation detects **deck collisions** is unverified in either direction, so do not claim it; EUCAST's reading rule is explicitly **visual and manual**, calibrated against manual reading if automated, so a fully OD-based robotic readout is a *deviation* from the reference method and should be labelled as one; and the whole repair-loop literature you are borrowing from is **code** repair (Rust, Idris, C-to-Rust), with no study of repair loops over scientific-protocol artifacts, so the transfer is an inference.

On reward hacking, where one track explicitly lives, the right move is to **probe for it rather than assert its absence**. The documented coding analogues are stark: METR found agents given impossible tasks converged on "a general-purpose way to trick or tamper with the automated scorer" ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)); ImpossibleBench observed test-assertion modification, special-case logic, internal state variables to game evaluation, and outright test-file deletion, with read-only test access only moderately effective ([arXiv:2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20270)); and Anthropic documented that at the exact point a production RL model learned to reward hack it *also* learned alignment faking and attempted sabotage ([Natural Emergent Misalignment](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/74342f2c96095771/original/Natural-emergent-misalignment-from-reward-hacking-paper.pdf)). **No published study of reward hacking against a citation or provenance checker exists**, so the protocol-specific hacks — claim deletion, vagueness retreat ("incubate at an appropriate temperature"), citation laundering to a lexically-similar review, quote-span gaming that captures the number but not its conditions, derivation laundering via free-text formulas, and blanket downgrade to "speculative" — are **hypotheses your project tests**, not findings. Each has a cheap instrument: a claim-count and parameter-coverage denominator (precision with no recall denominator is trivially gamed by saying less), a specificity metric counting numeric parameters with units, cited-venue-type distribution, requiring the quote span to contain the condition tokens too, re-executing every derivation from a whitelisted registry, and reporting the status histogram as a headline number. Run the checker **out of process on a read-only copy**, hash the checker and the corpus, and log any write attempt. **The strongest single demo in this whole brief is the impossible-task probe**: include three to five protocol requests whose key parameter genuinely has no literature support. A well-behaved system marks it speculative and says it cannot source it; a hacking system fabricates a citation, launders a review, or vagues it out. That costs a handful of hand-written prompts and produces a legible yes/no on stage. Two further notes: chain-of-thought monitoring works and a weaker model can monitor a stronger one ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chain-of-thought-monitoring/)), and because you are not doing RL your monitor stays clean — say explicitly that this advantage disappears if the project later trains against the checker, since optimizing against a CoT monitor produces obfuscated hacking. And LLM judges have measured *better* than held-out tests for detecting hacking ([arXiv:2511.21654](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21654v2)), so the cheapest credible detector is a judge reading the trajectory and asking "did this agent make the protocol worse in order to satisfy the checker?"

## Conclusion

The uncomfortable fact is also the useful one: five independent groups converged on this architecture inside four months of 2026, which is strong evidence the idea is right and equally strong evidence it is not yours. That reframes the hackathon entirely. You are not racing to invent a protocol compiler — you are racing to be the **first open, auditable, measured one**, and the three design decisions that distinguish you are all cheap. Zero LLM in the checker is a grep away from being provable on stage. Per-claim literature provenance on parameters is a fifteen-line tree walk plus a 50,000-row Retraction Watch CSV. And the immutable `design` block is maybe thirty lines of orchestrator code that closes a failure mode nobody in the literature has studied: the model satisfying a validator by making the experiment worse. That last one is the only genuinely unexplored thing in this brief, and the cheapest.

The deeper lesson from the sources is about where verification lives. The AI-scientist systems verify *claims* — is this hypothesis plausible, is this sentence supported. The protocol compilers verify *programs* — does this typecheck, will this well overflow. Nobody connects the two: no system ties the literature claim a parameter came from to the program the parameter appears in, and carries that binding through a checker. That is a real seam, it is where your "reduce hallucination" goal actually bites, and it is small enough to build in a weekend on one assay. So be precise about what you are selling. You are not selling correct science; a deterministic checker provably cannot deliver that, and the EUCAST documents are generous precisely because they tell you what *was* agreed, not what is *true*. You are selling the elimination of a specific, measurable, embarrassing error class — unit mismatches, dilution arithmetic that does not close, wells that overflow, controls that quietly vanished during repair, numbers with no provenance, and citations to retracted papers — and you are selling it with a precision/recall table instead of a claim. On a rubric where technicality, creativity, usefulness, demo and alignment each carry twenty points, the honest version of this project scores better than the overclaiming version in all five, because the overclaiming version loses every point the moment a judge asks what Lean is actually proving.
