# Assay Scope Selection for a Protocol-Generation + Static-Checker Hackathon Demo

Scope of these notes: evaluate broth microdilution antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) as the primary demo assay category, identify the freely available published standards and reference data a static checker could encode, assess the checkerboard/FICI extension, automation (Opentrons/PyLabRobot) fit, open data for grounding/validation, candidate "reproduce a paper result" targets, and score alternative assay scopes. Research conducted 2026-10-01; all factual claims carry a URL.

**Bottom line recommendation (stated up front for the report writer):** Primary = **EUCAST/ISO 20776-1 broth microdilution MIC determination for non-fastidious aerobic bacteria** (the exact method for "compare two approved antibiotics"). Backup 1 = **checkerboard (two-drug combination) microdilution with FIC index**, which is the same plate, same media, same inoculum, same readout, plus one published arithmetic index — this is the natural "go further" step rather than a separate scope. Backup 2 = **serial-dilution + standard-curve colorimetric protein assay (BCA/Bradford)** as a fallback if the team wants something with zero microbiology and a manufacturer-published numeric linear range.

## Key Question 1: Is broth microdilution AST the right primary candidate? Which governing documents are free, and what exactly can be encoded as range constraints?

### Takeaway
Broth microdilution is an unusually good fit: EUCAST publishes the *whole* parameter set as free PDFs (inoculum density with an explicit tolerance range, medium, temperature ± tolerance, incubation duration ± tolerance, atmosphere, agent-specific media modifications, reading rules, and validity criteria), and these are stated as numbers with tolerances — i.e. already in the shape a range checker wants. ISO 20776-1:2019 itself is paywalled, but EUCAST states its own recommendations are "in complete agreement" with ISO and restates the operative numbers, so the checker never needs the paid document.

**Encodable range constraints — directly quotable from the free EUCAST Sept 2026 document**
All of the following are from [EUCAST BMD recommendations Sept 2026](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/eucast/pdf/Document_Archive/bacteria/disk_diffusion_methodology/EUCAST_recommendations_for_broth_microdilution_MIC_determination_September_2026.pdf):
- **Inoculum density:** "The final inoculum should give a final cell number of 5x10^5 CFU/mL (target range 2x10^5 to 8x10^5 CFU/mL); viable counts should be performed to ensure that the final inoculum is within the specified range (see ISO 20776-1)." → a hard interval constraint with a nominal target.
- **Medium (non-fastidious):** Cation-Adjusted Mueller-Hinton Broth (CAMHB) for Enterobacterales, *Staphylococcus* spp., *Enterococcus* spp., *Pseudomonas* spp., *Acinetobacter* spp., *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia*, *Vibrio* spp., *Achromobacter* spp., *Aeromonas* spp. (Table 1a).
- **Medium (fastidious):** MH-F broth = "Cation-Adjusted Mueller-Hinton Broth with 5% lysed horse blood and 20 mg/L beta-NAD" for *Streptococcus* spp., *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Corynebacterium* spp., etc. (Table 1b).
- **Incubation:** "Sealed panels, air, 35±1ºC, 18±2h" for essentially all CAMHB organisms (Table 1a) → two ± tolerance constraints plus an atmosphere enum plus a plate-sealing requirement.
- **Documented exceptions the checker should special-case:** *Aeromonas salmonicida* sp. *salmonicida* = 22±1ºC, 48±2h; *Brucella melitensis* = 35±1ºC, 48±2h; *Campylobacter coli/jejuni* = "Microaerobic environment, 41±1ºC, 24±1h" with "Panels must be covered with a perforated seal"; several fastidious genera allow "40-44 h incubation if insufficient growth after 16-20 h".
- **Agent-dependent duration override:** "24 h incubation for glycopeptides and for oxacillin when tested for *Staphylococcus* spp." (footnote 3, Table 1a); "24 h incubation for glycopeptides" (footnote 3, Table 1b).
- **Agent-dependent medium modifications (Table 2)** — a clean rule table for the checker: oxacillin → "Medium supplemented to a final concentration of 20 g/L NaCl when tested for *Staphylococcus* spp."; cefiderocol → iron-depleted CAMHB; lipoglycopeptides (dalbavancin, telavancin, oritavancin) → "Medium supplemented with 0.002% polysorbate-80"; tigecycline → "Medium prepared fresh on the day of use"; daptomycin → "Medium supplemented with 50 mg/L Ca2+".
- **Reading rule:** "Most MIC endpoints are read at the lowest concentration of the agent that completely inhibits visible growth, as stated in ISO 20776-1."
- **Agent-specific reading overrides (Table 3):** bacteriostatic agents (macrolides, lincosamides, streptogramins, tetracyclines, oxazolidinones, phenicols, fusidic acid) → "Disregard pinpoint growth (tiny buttons) when trailing growth occurs"; cefiderocol → MIC is the first well where growth is reduced to a button <1 mm or light haze, and "The positive control should show strong growth in the form of a button of >2 mm or heavy turbidity".
- **Inoculum provenance rule:** fastidious organisms "normally need at least two passages on suitable agar media when retrieved from freezer storage"; the dilution needed from a McFarland 0.5 suspension depends on species and delivery method. McFarland 0.5 is given a numeric equivalence for *B. anthracis*: "the theoretical CFU/mL of a McF 0.5 solution (1-2x10^8 CFU/mL)".

**Encodable validity / control constraints — from the free EUCAST reading guide v5.0**
All from [Reading guide BMD v5.0 2024](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/src/media/PDFs/EUCAST_files/MIC_testing/Reading_guide_BMD_v_5.0_2024.pdf):
- "Results are only valid when the following criteria are met: **Sufficient growth**, i.e. obvious button or definite turbidity, in the positive growth control. **Pure culture** ... Check for purity by subculturing from the growth-control well immediately after inoculation onto a non-selective agar plate for simultaneous incubation. **Correct inoculum 5 x 10^5 CFU/mL**."
- **Verification arithmetic the checker can literally re-derive:** "Viable colony counts can be performed by removing 10 µL from the growth-control well or tube immediately after inoculation and diluting in 10 mL of saline. Mix and spread 100 µL onto a non-selective agar plate. After incubation, the number of colonies should be approximately 20-80." (This is a beautiful target for a dilution-arithmetic checker: 10 µL into 10 mL = 1:1001 ≈ 1:1000; 100 µL plated → 5x10^5 CFU/mL × 10^-3 × 0.1 mL = 50 CFU, centre of the stated 20–80 window.)
- "Read the MIC as the lowest concentration of antimicrobial agent that completely inhibits growth of the organism as detected by the unaided eye." "Results should be read manually. The use of a mirror may facilitate reading. If an automated reader or camera system is used, it must be calibrated to manual reading."
- Turbidity/haze without a pellet (common for *Pseudomonas*, *Acinetobacter*) "should be regarded as growth"; haemolysis with turbidity or pellet in MH-F broth "should be regarded as growth".
- **Skipped-well rule (a genuine structural check):** "When a single skipped well occurs, retest the isolate or read the highest MIC value"; "Do not report results for antimicrobial agents for which there is more than one skipped well."
- "Make sure that MIC values for relevant Quality Control strains are within acceptable ranges before reporting results for clinical isolates" and interpret into S/I/R using the current EUCAST breakpoint tables.
- Broth microdilution "is the reference method for antimicrobial susceptibility testing of rapidly growing aerobic bacteria, except for mecillinam and fosfomycin, where agar dilution is the reference method" → an explicit *negative* constraint the checker can enforce (reject a BMD protocol for fosfomycin/mecillinam).

**Plate layout, volumes, dilution series, replicates — from a peer-reviewed open STAR Protocols paper**
From "Antimicrobial susceptibility testing to evaluate minimum inhibitory concentration values of clinically relevant antibiotics", STAR Protocols — [PMC10448204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448204/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666166723004793):
- Layout: 8 antibiotics in rows A–H; **10 two-fold dilution steps in columns 1–10**; **column 11 = positive growth control (bacteria, no antibiotic)**; **column 12 = negative/sterility control (media only)**.
- Volumes: add 50 µL of 2× bacterial inoculum to all wells except column 12; 50 µL media to the sterility control wells → 100 µL final per well.
- Inoculum: 2× inoculum at 10^6 CFU/mL → ~5×10^5 CFU/mL final per well; "actual 2× bacterial inoculum is within 3-fold of target" verified by serial dilution and colony counts.
- Incubation: 20 h at 37 °C; CAMHB in ambient atmosphere, DMEM in 5% CO2.
- **Replicates: 3 biological replicates.**
- Readout: visual turbidity; "The MIC is the lowest antibiotic concentration that inhibits bacterial growth."
- Stock solutions: "Antibiotic stock solutions are typically solubilized in deionized H2O (filtered and autoclaved)" at 10 mg/mL, with ethanol/methanol/acetone as alternatives for water-insoluble agents. Manual pipetting; no robot specified in that paper.

### Inferences
- The free EUCAST corpus is *already* a constraint table rather than prose: `5×10^5 CFU/mL [2×10^5, 8×10^5]`, `35±1 °C`, `18±2 h`, `CAMHB | MH-F broth`, `sealed | perforated seal`, `20 g/L NaCl if oxacillin ∧ Staphylococcus`. That is unusually low friction for a 24–48 h build compared with assay families whose "standard" is a vendor kit insert.
- The 10 µL→10 mL→100 µL viable-count instruction and the stated 20–80 colony window give the checker a *self-consistency theorem* to prove, not just a lookup — the best possible demo for a "dilution arithmetic" checker, because the published standard supplies both the procedure and the expected numeric outcome.
- Because the full plate has only 12 columns, "10 two-fold dilutions + growth control + sterility control" exactly saturates a 96-well plate. A labware-capacity checker therefore has a non-trivial, naturally-arising constraint to enforce (adding an 11th dilution step forces the loss of a mandated control well — exactly the class of error the demo wants to catch).
- Since the EUCAST QC tables footnote their ranges as CLSI M100-S35-derived but EUCAST-validated, the team can honestly say the checker encodes internationally harmonised QC ranges without touching a paywalled document.
- The "citation for every parameter" requirement is satisfiable at document+table+footnote granularity for essentially every parameter, which is rare.

### Gaps
- I could not read ISO 20776-1:2019 itself (paywalled), so I cannot confirm ISO's own wording on well volume, number of dilution steps, or replicate counts. EUCAST's restatement plus the STAR Protocols paper cover the practical numbers. A 2006 first-edition PDF is circulating on a third-party university server ([copy at rc.med.sumdu.edu.ua](https://rc.med.sumdu.edu.ua/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/ISO_20776_1_2006_EN.pdf.pdf)) — it is **superseded** and of uncertain authorisation; I would not build on it or cite it in a demo.
- EUCAST's media-preparation document ("Media preparation for EUCAST disk diffusion testing and for determination of MIC values by the broth microdilution method") is referenced by the Sept 2026 recommendation but my direct URL guess 404'd; it is reachable from the EUCAST site navigation. I did not capture its exact cation concentrations (Ca2+/Mg2+ ranges for CAMHB), which would be a valuable extra constraint.
- EUCAST does not, in the documents I read, mandate a specific *replicate count* for routine MIC determination (replicates = 3 biological comes from the STAR Protocols paper, not from EUCAST). Flag this distinction rather than attributing "n=3" to EUCAST.

## Key Question 2: Does this extend to checkerboard / combination testing with a published interaction index and cut-offs?

### Takeaway
Yes, and it is the single cleanest "and it generalizes / we went further" move: the checkerboard is the same medium, inoculum, incubation and readout, arranged as a 2-D grid on the same 96-well plate, with one extra published arithmetic index (FICI) and conventional cut-offs of ≤0.5 for synergy and >4.0 for antagonism. Important caveat for honesty in the demo: those cut-offs are *conventional and editorially argued*, not an ISO/EUCAST standard.

### Cited Findings
- The conventional interpretation: "FICI ranges that define 'synergy' (FICI ≤ 0.5), 'antagonism' (FICI > 4.0) and 'no interaction' (FICI > 0.5–4.0)", from F.C. Odds, "Synergy, antagonism, and what the chequerboard puts between them", *Journal of Antimicrobial Chemotherapy* 52(1):1 (August 2003) — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Synergy,-antagonism,-and-what-the-chequerboard-puts-Odds/d4be3d37758841bf3023026c9e340386d6ab5580); [citation record](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=1838537)
- The rationale for those particular cut-offs, i.e. why 0.5 and 4 and not 1: "because of the twofold drug dilution scheme and the 1-dilution error of single-drug susceptibility testing methods used for FIC index determination, cutoffs of 0.5 and 4 were suggested for defining additivity/indifference" — Meletiadis et al., "Defining Fractional Inhibitory Concentration Index Cutoffs for Additive Interactions...", *Antimicrobial Agents and Chemotherapy* — [PMC2812160](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2812160/); [journals.asm.org full text](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aac.00999-09)
- The older primary methodological reference for the index itself: "The fractional inhibitory concentration (FIC) index as a measure of synergy", *Journal of Antimicrobial Chemotherapy* 11(5):427 — [JAC PDF](https://academic.oup.com/jac/article-pdf/11/5/427/2208905/11-5-427.pdf)
- ASM/AAC has published repeated editorial guidance on interpreting checkerboard synergy data, useful as a second standard-setting voice — [AAC 2006 editorial, 50(2):825–826](https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/aac.50.2.825-826.2006); [AAC 2011, 55(9):4447–4450](https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/aac.01681-10)
- Checkerboard mechanics in plain terms: titrate one agent in serial dilution across the plate while titrating the second in serial dilution down the plate, allowing determination of synergy between the two compounds; in some checkerboard variants MIC is defined as the lowest concentration reducing growth by >80% — [search-surfaced description of checkerboard methodology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355272169_New_and_simplified_method_for_drug_combination_studies_by_checkerboard_assay) (secondary source; treat the >80% definition as assay-specific, not as the EUCAST visual-inhibition definition)
- Alternative/continuous analyses exist if the team wants a stretch goal: a Hill-function-based approach to checkerboard synergy analysis — [PMC11839125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11839125/); and a simplified physical layout, CombiANT, for antibiotic interaction testing — [PLoS Biology, CombiANT](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3000856)
- Open-source MIC data analysis tooling already exists (DANAMIC, "Data analyzer of minimum inhibitory concentrations") — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666166722006621)

### Inferences
- FICI is an ideal checker target because it is *pure arithmetic over values the protocol already declares*: FICI = (MIC_A in combination / MIC_A alone) + (MIC_B in combination / MIC_B alone). A static checker can verify (a) that both single-agent MIC wells exist on the plate (the FICI is undefined without them — a very common real design error), (b) that the dilution grid actually spans each agent's expected MIC range so the combination MIC is bracketed rather than off-scale, and (c) that the reported classification matches the computed index against the ≤0.5 / >4.0 cut-offs.
- The 2-D checkerboard immediately creates a *labware capacity* conflict on a 96-well plate: an 8×8 grid of two agents consumes 64 wells and leaves 32, which must still hold single-agent rows/columns plus growth and sterility controls. This is the strongest possible demonstration of a capacity checker with a real, published-method-driven constraint.
- The cut-offs' stated justification (the ±1 two-fold-dilution measurement error) is itself a *units/precision* argument. That lets the team demo a precision check: a protocol claiming to resolve a FICI of 0.45 vs 0.55 from a two-fold dilution series is not supported by the method's resolution. This is a genuinely novel-feeling check that follows directly from a citable source.
- Be explicit in the demo that FICI cut-offs are convention from an editorial plus derived analyses, not from ISO 20776-1 or EUCAST. Over-claiming here is the main credibility risk in this scope.

### Gaps
- Neither EUCAST nor ISO publishes a standardised checkerboard/combination method for bacteria that I could find; combination AST is methodologically unstandardised relative to single-agent MIC. The checker must therefore cite journal convention (Odds 2003 et seq.), not a standards body, for the combination layer.
- I could not verify a stable publisher URL for the Odds 2003 editorial itself (OUP full text was not surfaced); the Semantic Scholar and citation records above confirm the content and the cut-off values.

## Key Question 3: Automation fit — published Opentrons / PyLabRobot protocols for microdilution or checkerboard setup

### Takeaway
There is no official Opentrons Protocol Library entry specifically titled "broth microdilution MIC" or "checkerboard" that I could confirm, but there *is* an officially maintained, parameterised serial-dilution protocol for both Flex and OT-2 that is the exact primitive a microdilution plate needs, plus Opentrons' own simulation tooling and third-party GitHub repos doing pharmaceutical serial dilution on OT-2. PyLabRobot is a published, hardware-agnostic Python API with a journal paper. Compiling to a real hardware API and dry-running in a simulator is therefore realistic inside 24–48 h; shipping a *validated-against-a-published-MIC-protocol* robot run is not.

### Cited Findings
- Opentrons maintains a customizable serial dilution protocol for the Flex: "a simple serial dilution across a 96-well plate using either a single-channel or 8-channel pipette", with user-settable **Dilution Factor** and **Total Mixing Volume** parameters — [Opentrons FAQ: example serial dilution protocol for Flex](https://opentrons.com/archives/faq/can-i-see-an-example-protocol-for-automated-serial-dilution-on-the-flex); protocol page [library.opentrons.com/p/customizable_serial_dilution_flex](https://library.opentrons.com/p/customizable_serial_dilution_flex) (HTTP 200 confirmed 2026-10-01; page is client-rendered so I could not scrape its labware/pipette list)
- The OT-2 equivalent page also resolves: [library.opentrons.com/p/customizable-serial-dilution](https://library.opentrons.com/p/customizable-serial-dilution) (HTTP 200 confirmed; client-rendered)
- Opentrons describes the Protocol Library as "an open, searchable repository of downloadable and configurable protocols", covering nucleic acid extraction, NGS library prep, protein purification, ELISA and cell-based assays — [Intro to Protocol Library](https://opentrons.com/intro-to-protocol-library); [opentrons.com/protocols](https://opentrons.com/protocols); category listing [Basic Pipetting](https://protocol-delivery.protocols.opentrons.com/categories/Basic%20Pipetting/)
- Opentrons ships protocol visualization/simulation, which is what makes a generated protocol dry-runnable without hardware — [Opentrons: Protocol Visualization & Simulation](https://opentrons.com/software/protocol-visualization-simulation); [Opentrons Software](https://opentrons.com/software)
- Third-party, simulator-tested OT-2 serial dilution code in a regulated-style workflow: "Simulator-tested Python/Opentrons workflow for automated drug-product sample preparation, serial dilution, quality control, and validation-aware liquid handling" — eight samples transferred to a 96-well plate and diluted across six concentrations, with automated diluent dispensing, a six-point 1:10 serial dilution and a fresh-tip strategy — [github.com/chumawinnie/opentrons-drug-product-automation](https://github.com/chumawinnie/opentrons-drug-product-automation)
- An academic Opentrons protocol collection (University of Copenhagen EvoGenomics) demonstrating that labs publish OT-2 protocol libraries on GitHub — [github.com/anttonalberdi/EvoGenOT](https://github.com/anttonalberdi/EvoGenOT)
- PyLabRobot: "a hardware agnostic, pure Python library for liquid handling robots, plate readers, pumps, scales, heater shakers, and other lab automation equipment"; cross-platform; supports atomic commands run interactively in Jupyter/REPL; open source; published in *Device* (2023) as "PyLabRobot: An open-source, hardware-agnostic interface for liquid-handling robots and accessories" — [github.com/PyLabRobot/pylabrobot](https://github.com/PyLabRobot/pylabrobot); [PyLabRobot org](https://github.com/PyLabRobot)
- A published, robot-assisted high-throughput MIC workflow exists in the literature: a robot creates the testing plates, inhibition is determined on a microplate reader, and Python assigns MIC values — [bioRxiv 2022.10.21.513098](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2022/10/21/2022.10.21.513098.full.pdf)
- A droplet-based automated antibiotic-combination screening platform (robotic-printed combinatorial droplets) shows the combination layout is an active automation target — [PMC10985126](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10985126/)

### Inferences
- The compile target should be the **Opentrons Python Protocol API v2** with a two-fold serial dilution across columns 1–10 plus explicit growth (col 11) and sterility (col 12) wells — i.e. the published Opentrons serial dilution protocol with `dilution_factor = 2` and a plate layout the checker constrains. For the hackathon that means the "generated protocol compiles and simulates" claim is backed by official vendor example code rather than something the team invents.
- Dry-run evidence is the credible deliverable for the hardware/automation track: show the generated protocol simulating cleanly, plus the checker rejecting mutated variants (11 dilutions → overwrites the growth control; 250 µL total volume → exceeds standard 96-well working capacity; wrong tip/volume pairing → pipette range violation).
- PyLabRobot is the better choice only if the team wants hardware-agnosticism as part of the pitch; Opentrons has better odds of a clean simulator run in 24–48 h because the example protocol is first-party and parameterised.
- "Equipment ranges" checks have obvious concrete content here: pipette min/max volume per channel, plate max working volume, incubator temperature setpoint vs 35±1 °C, and plate-reader wavelength if an OD readout is added.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the existence of an Opentrons Protocol Library entry for broth microdilution MIC or checkerboard specifically; both the library search page and the individual protocol pages are JavaScript-rendered and returned no scrapeable text. The team should search library.opentrons.com directly before claiming "no existing MIC protocol".
- I did not verify the URL for the `opentrons_simulate` CLI documentation, so do not cite a specific docs page for it without checking.
- The EUCAST reading rule is explicitly *visual, manual* reading, calibrated against manual reading if automated. A fully automated OD-based readout is therefore a deviation from the reference method, and the demo should say so rather than imply the robot run is the reference method.

## Key Question 4: Open data for grounding and validating outputs — which are API-accessible?

### Takeaway
Three of the four needed data layers are genuinely open and API-accessible today: PubChem PUG-REST (no key, verified live), ChEMBL REST (CC-BY-SA, verified live), and the EUCAST MIC distribution database (free web, bulk access via Excel templates rather than a documented API). DrugBank is the weak link — license-gated. Solubility and recommended stock solvent are the thinnest data layer and will need a hand-curated table for the two demo drugs.

### Cited Findings
- **PubChem PUG-REST — verified working with no API key** on 2026-10-01: `GET https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug/compound/name/ciprofloxacin/property/MolecularWeight,XLogP,ConnectivitySMILES,IUPACName/JSON` returned HTTP 200 with CID 2764, MolecularWeight 331.34, XLogP -1.1, IUPACName "1-cyclopropyl-6-fluoro-4-oxo-7-piperazin-1-ylquinoline-3-carboxylic acid" (own live test; documentation hub: [PubChem PUG-REST docs](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/docs/pug-rest))
- **ChEMBL REST — verified working** on 2026-10-01: `GET https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/molecule/CHEMBL8.json` returned HTTP 200 with ATC classifications J01MA02, S03AA07, S01AE03, S02AA15 (ciprofloxacin) and a black-box-warning flag (own live test). Interactive docs: [ChEMBL data web services docs](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/docs) (HTTP 200, title "REST API"); [ChEMBL web services interface documentation](https://chembl.gitbook.io/chembl-interface-documentation/web-services); [GLaDOS-docs web services](https://github.com/chembl/GLaDOS-docs/blob/master/web-services/chembl-data-web-services.md); methods paper [Nucleic Acids Research 43:W612](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/43/W1/W612/2467881) / [PMC4489243](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4489243/)
- ChEMBL data are licensed CC-BY-SA, i.e. permissive — [ChEMBL web services docs](https://chembl.gitbook.io/chembl-interface-documentation/web-services); [ChEMBL (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChEMBL)
- **DrugBank** is "available for commercial and academic research and is also accessible via a clinical API"; academic users "may apply for a free license for certain use cases and all other users require a paid license" — [DrugBank](https://go.drugbank.com/) (licensing statement surfaced via search; treat as medium confidence and verify the exact academic terms before depending on it in a 48 h build)
- **EUCAST MIC distribution database**: freely available at [mic.eucast.org](https://mic.eucast.org/) (HTTP 200 confirmed, title "MIC EUCAST"), with a choice of "MIC" or "Disk diffusion"; hub page [EUCAST: MIC and Zone Distributions, ECOFFs](https://www.eucast.org/bacteria/mic-and-zone-distributions-ecoffs/). It presents MIC and inhibition-zone-diameter distributions per organism/agent as tables and histograms, and where enough acceptable distributions exist, an ECOFF with confidence intervals is listed beneath each graph. The database "was created by Gunnar Kahlmeter for EUCAST from 2002 and onwards", is curated by Kahlmeter and John Turnidge, and "now consists of more than 40,000 distributions" — [mic.eucast.org](https://mic.eucast.org/); [EUCAST: How to make use of EUCAST MIC and zone distributions](https://www.eucast.org/news-detail/how-to-make-use-of-eucast-mic-and-zone-distributions/)
- Bulk/contributed data move via Excel templates rather than a documented REST API; templates are downloadable from the EUCAST MIC distributions pages — [EUCAST: MIC and Zone Distributions, ECOFFs](https://www.eucast.org/bacteria/mic-and-zone-distributions-ecoffs/)
- How ECOFFs are derived (useful if the checker wants to sanity-check that a chosen dilution range brackets the wild-type distribution): [EUCAST SOP 10.2, MIC distributions and ECOFF setting](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/src/media/PDFs/EUCAST_files/EUCAST_SOPs/2021/EUCAST_SOP_10.2_MIC_distributions_and_epidemiological_cut-off_value__ECOFF__setting_20211202.pdf); [Information on how distributions and ECOFFs are generated](https://www.eucast.org/mic-and-zone-distributions-and-ecoffs/information-on-how-distributions-and-ecoffs-are-generated); review: "How to: ECOFFs—the why, the how, and the don'ts of EUCAST epidemiological cutoff values", *Clinical Microbiology and Infection* — [CMI full text](https://www.clinicalmicrobiologyandinfection.org/article/S1198-743X(22)00101-X/fulltext)
- **Measured drug-interaction data, browsable and open**: the Brochado et al. 2018 combination screen is browsable in a live interactive interface — [apps.embl.de/combact](https://apps.embl.de/combact/) (HTTP 200 confirmed 2026-10-01, title "Combinatorial Screen Explorer")
- **Stock-solution solvent guidance** that is publication-grade rather than vendor-only: "Antibiotic stock solutions are typically solubilized in deionized H2O (filtered and autoclaved)" at 10 mg/mL, with ethanol/methanol/acetone alternatives for water-insoluble agents — [STAR Protocols, PMC10448204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448204/)

### Inferences
- A workable grounding stack for a 48 h build: PubChem PUG-REST for molecular weight (needed for any mol/L ↔ mg/L unit conversion in the checker) and SMILES/identifiers; ChEMBL for drug identity, ATC class and published activity records; EUCAST QC tables for the expected MIC window of the reference strain; mic.eucast.org for the wild-type MIC distribution and ECOFF to check the dilution series actually spans the relevant range; and a small hand-curated solubility/solvent table for the two demo antibiotics with a citation per row.
- PubChem's MolecularWeight field is the load-bearing piece for the units checker: "2 mg/L" vs "6 µM" of ciprofloxacin is only reconcilable with MW 331.34, and getting that from an API with a URL per value is exactly the "citation for every parameter" story.
- Experimental aqueous solubility and stock stability are the least API-friendly fields. PubChem exposes experimental properties through PUG-View annotations rather than the computed-property endpoint, so plan for a two-step lookup and expect gaps; do not promise automated solubility retrieval in the demo.
- DrugBank should be treated as optional. Nothing in the demo needs it if PubChem + ChEMBL + EUCAST are wired up, and its license gate is a bad dependency on a 48 h clock.

### Gaps
- I did not verify PubChem PUG-REST's documented rate limits or confirm which experimental solubility/stability fields are retrievable via PUG-View, because the PubChem docs pages are client-rendered and returned no text. Plan for throttling (PubChem publishes a request-rate policy) and verify before building a scraper.
- No documented REST API for mic.eucast.org was found; access appears to be interactive web plus Excel downloads. If the team needs bulk MIC distributions programmatically, budget scraping time or use the Excel templates.
- I found no single curated open dataset of *checkerboard FICI values* across antibiotic pairs. The closest is the Brochado/Cacace interaction screens (next section), which use their own interaction scores rather than FICI.

## Key Question 5: "Reproduce a key result from a paper, then go beyond it" candidates inside this scope

### Takeaway
Two families work in a hackathon timebox. The ML-on-molecules family (Stokes 2020 Cell; Wong 2024 Nature) has published code, published checkpoints and a Zenodo deposit, so "reproduce a prediction/ranking" is hours-scale. The large bacterial drug-interaction screens (Brochado 2018; Cacace 2023) are better as *validation corpora* — re-derive a known synergy call and have the generated protocol propose the plate that would confirm it — than as full recomputations. Protocol-generation benchmarks (BioProBench, LAB-Bench/ProtocolQA) are the natural quantitative scoreboard for the checker itself.

### Cited Findings

**A. Large published drug-interaction screens in bacteria**
- Brochado et al., "Species-specific activity of antibacterial drug combinations", *Nature* 559:259–263 (2018): profiled "almost 3,000 dose-resolved combinations of antibiotics, human-targeted drugs and food additives in six strains from three Gram-negative pathogens" (*E. coli*, *S. enterica* ser. Typhimurium, *P. aeruginosa*); "more than 70% of the drug–drug interactions detected are species-specific and 20% display strain specificity" — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0278-9); plain-language summary [EMBL news](https://www.embl.org/news/science/combining-antibiotics-changes-their-effectiveness/); data browsable at [apps.embl.de/combact](https://apps.embl.de/combact/); author-accepted manuscript open in a repository — [University of Birmingham PDF](https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/53705911/Ana_Rita_Brochado_et_al_Species_specific_activity_of_antibacterial_Nature_2018.pdf)
- Cacace et al., "Systematic analysis of drug combinations against Gram-positive bacteria", *Nature Microbiology* 8(11):2196–2212 (2023): "profiled approximately 8,000 combinations of 65 antibacterial drugs" against *B. subtilis*, *S. aureus* and *S. pneumoniae* using an automated high-throughput screen; "identified ten times more novel interactions in the pathogen *S. aureus*, including 150 synergies"; ticagrelor synergized with cationic antibiotics by modifying *S. aureus* surface charge — [Nature Microbiology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-023-01486-9); [EMBL news](https://www.embl.org/news/science/fighting-antimicrobial-resistance-with-new-drug-combinations/); [Groningen repository record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/systematic-analysis-of-drug-combinations-against-gram-positive-ba/)
- Adjacent, same-lab-style resource with chemical genetics: "Systematic mapping of antibiotic cross-resistance and collateral sensitivity with chemical genetics", *Nature Microbiology* — [Nature Microbiology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01857-w)
- Reviews framing the combination-therapy problem for a pitch: "Drug combinations targeting antibiotic resistance" — [npj Antimicrobials & Resistance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44259-024-00047-2)

**B. Machine learning on antibacterial compounds**
- Stokes et al., "A Deep Learning Approach to Antibiotic Discovery", *Cell* 180:688–702 (2020): trained a deep neural network to predict antibacterial molecules; discovered halicin from the Drug Repurposing Hub, "structurally divergent from conventional antibiotics". Code: Chemprop at [github.com/swansonk14/chemprop](https://github.com/swansonk14/chemprop); public web predictor at [chemprop.csail.mit.edu](http://chemprop.csail.mit.edu/); full text PDF [Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/pdf/S0092-8674(20)30102-1.pdf) and [Collins Lab copy](https://www.collinslab.mit.edu/s/cell_stokes.pdf); an independent re-implementation exists at [Ersilia eos4e40](https://github.com/ersilia-os/eos4e40); curated model records at [OmicsDI MODEL2404080001](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/biomodels/MODEL2404080001) and [BioModels](https://biomodels.org/MODEL2404080001)
- Wong et al., "Discovery of a structural class of antibiotics with explainable deep learning", *Nature* 626:177–185 (2024): explainable, substructure-based deep learning over chemical space; one identified structural class "was selective against methicillin-resistant *S. aureus* (MRSA) and vancomycin-resistant enterococci, evaded substantial resistance, and reduced bacterial titres in mouse models". Model predictions and Chemprop checkpoints for antibiotic activity, cytotoxicity and proton-motive-force alteration are public — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06887-8); code [github.com/felixjwong/antibioticsai](https://github.com/felixjwong/antibioticsai); checkpoints [Zenodo 10095879](https://zenodo.org/records/10095879); [PubMed 38123686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38123686/)
- Companion *Nature Protocols* paper making the pipeline reproducible step-by-step: "An explainable deep learning platform for molecular discovery" — [Nature Protocols](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41596-024-01084-x); code [github.com/felixjwong/protocol](https://github.com/felixjwong/protocol)
- A 2025 deep reinforcement learning platform for antibiotic discovery (recent, for a "state of the art" framing) — [arXiv 2509.18153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18153)

**C. Protocol-generation / protocol-reasoning benchmarks**
- BioProBench: built on BioProCorpus, "a foundational collection of 22,413 human-written protocols", with five tasks — protocol QA, step ordering, error correction, protocol generation, and protocol reasoning. Reported LLM weakness: "Performance plummets on Step Ordering (ORD-EM ~50%) and Protocol Generation (GEN-BLEU <15%)", with "systemic weaknesses in handling causal logic, quantitative precision, and structured generation" — [arXiv 2505.07889](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07889); code [github.com/YuyangSunshine/bioprobench](https://github.com/YuyangSunshine/bioprobench)
- ProtocolQA: "a multiple-choice benchmark on troubleshooting failed experimental outcomes from common biological laboratory protocols"; a component of LAB-Bench, whose successor LAB-Bench 2 adds free-response ProtocolQA2 — [ProtocolQA leaderboard](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/protocolqa); [discussion of LAB-Bench results](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/5EhJhMAdjncpSzzEL/llms-outperform-experts-on-challenging-biology-benchmarks)
- Newer protocol-agent and protocol-modification benchmarks: "A Self-Evolving Agentic System for Automated Generation and Execution of Biological Protocols" — [arXiv 2606.31763](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31763); "BenchBench-Protocol: Evaluating Real-World Wet-Lab Protocol Reasoning and Modification" — [arXiv 2608.23898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23898)

### Inferences
Ranked for a 24–48 h "reproduce then go beyond" sponsor challenge, with honest time estimates:

1. **Wong 2024 (Nature) — best single target.** Reproduce: load the published Chemprop checkpoints from the Zenodo deposit and regenerate the paper's activity/cytotoxicity predictions for the released compound set, checking they match the deposited predictions. Plausible time: a few hours, mostly environment setup, because checkpoints and prediction files are both published. Go beyond: feed the top-ranked predicted hits into the protocol generator, have it emit an EUCAST-compliant broth microdilution plate (plus a checkerboard against a comparator) and have the static checker certify it — i.e. "the ML paper ends at a ranked list; we emit the next experiment, machine-checked against ISO/EUCAST."
2. **Stokes 2020 (Cell) — equally fast, more famous.** Reproduce: run the public Chemprop model / web predictor over the Drug Repurposing Hub and recover halicin's high rank. Plausible time: hours; the web predictor alone makes a live demo possible in minutes. Go beyond: same as above — generate and statically verify the confirmatory MIC protocol, with the EUCAST QC strain ranges as the built-in positive control.
3. **Brochado 2018 / Cacace 2023 — best *validation corpus*, risky as a recomputation.** Reproduce: pull a specific published species-specific synergy (e.g. a pair called synergistic in *E. coli* but not in *P. aeruginosa*) out of the Combinatorial Screen Explorer and reproduce the interaction call. Plausible time: hours *if* the underlying matrices download cleanly; days if the raw screen data require reprocessing from supplementary files. Go beyond: convert each published interaction call into a machine-checkable checkerboard plate design, run the FICI arithmetic checker over it, and report how many published pairs the generator can propose a valid confirmatory plate for. Risk to flag: these papers use their own Bliss/Loewe-style interaction scores, **not** FICI, so "reproduce the metric" is not a one-liner.
4. **BioProBench / ProtocolQA — best quantitative scoreboard for the checker itself.** Reproduce: run the published protocol-generation and error-correction splits and reproduce the reported weak baseline numbers (GEN-BLEU <15%, ORD-EM ~50%). Plausible time: hours for a subset. Go beyond: show that adding the static checker as a verifier/repair loop improves the error-correction and generation scores on the AST subset. This is the most *defensible* "we measured it" claim available, and it directly supports the "generalizes" story because the benchmark spans 22,413 protocols from many assay families.

### Gaps
- I could not extract the Brochado 2018 data-availability statement verbatim. My PDF text extraction only recovered the repository cover page, and the Nature page redirects through an identity provider. The team must check the paper's Data Availability section and the Combinatorial Screen Explorer download options before committing to it as a recomputation target; I therefore cannot state how the raw matrices are distributed or under what licence.
- Likewise for Cacace 2023: I have the abstract-level numbers from search results but did not read the Data Availability statement. Treat "open access therefore data available" as unverified.
- I did not verify the LAB-Bench primary paper URL (FutureHouse); only the leaderboard page and a secondary discussion.
- Two of the benchmark arXiv entries (2606.31763, 2608.23898) are very recent 2026 preprints surfaced only through search snippets; I did not read them, so their claims should be checked before citing specifics.

## Key Question 6: Scoring alternative scopes

### Takeaway
Broth microdilution wins on the combination of (a) a free, numeric, internationally harmonised standard, (b) mandatory control wells and QC strain ranges that give the checker ground truth, and (c) a one-plate demo that is visually obvious. BCA/Bradford scores well on checkability but has no interaction/control-condition richness and no QC-strain analogue; qPCR has an excellent reporting standard (MIQE) but it is a *reporting checklist* rather than numeric ranges; buffer prep is the easiest to check but has almost no "required control conditions" to find; viability assays, ELISA and enzyme kinetics are either vendor-kit-defined or analysis-heavy.

### Cited Findings and per-scope assessment

Scoring key: 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) on standardization, parameter checkability, robot compatibility, demo clarity, data availability. Scores are my judgement; the supporting facts are cited.

**Primary — Broth microdilution AST (MIC) / checkerboard.** Standardization 5, checkability 5, robot compatibility 4, demo clarity 5, data availability 5.
- Free standard with numeric tolerances and agent-specific overrides ([EUCAST Sept 2026](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/eucast/pdf/Document_Archive/bacteria/disk_diffusion_methodology/EUCAST_recommendations_for_broth_microdilution_MIC_determination_September_2026.pdf)); free validity criteria and reading rules ([Reading guide v5.0](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/src/media/PDFs/EUCAST_files/MIC_testing/Reading_guide_BMD_v_5.0_2024.pdf)); free per-strain expected MIC windows ([QC tables v15.0](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/eucast/pdf/disk_test_documents/archive/v_15.0_EUCAST_QC_tables_routine_and_extended_QC.pdf)); free breakpoints for interpretation ([v16.1](https://www.eucast.org/fileadmin/eucast/pdf/breakpoints/v_16.1_Breakpoint_Tables.pdf)); 40,000+ open MIC distributions ([mic.eucast.org](https://mic.eucast.org/)); vendor-maintained serial dilution robot protocol ([Opentrons Flex serial dilution](https://library.opentrons.com/p/customizable_serial_dilution_flex)); mandated growth and sterility control wells in a published layout ([PMC10448204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448204/)).

**Backup 1 — Checkerboard / FICI (same family).** Standardization 3, checkability 5, robot compatibility 4, demo clarity 5, data availability 3. Strong on checkability (pure arithmetic with published cut-offs — [Odds 2003 record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Synergy,-antagonism,-and-what-the-chequerboard-puts-Odds/d4be3d37758841bf3023026c9e340386d6ab5580); [Meletiadis, PMC2812160](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2812160/)) but weaker on standardization because no standards body publishes the method; reference interaction data exist but use other metrics ([apps.embl.de/combact](https://apps.embl.de/combact/)).

**Backup 2 — Serial dilution + standard curve, colorimetric protein assay (Bradford/BCA).** Standardization 3, checkability 4, robot compatibility 5, demo clarity 4, data availability 2.
- Manufacturer-published numeric working ranges that a checker can encode: Pierce BCA has "a linear working range for BSA of 20 to 2000 μg/mL for the standard protocol"; the classic BCA range is 200–1,000 µg/mL; standard protocol = 37 °C for 30 min with absorbance read at 562 nm; "It is necessary to create a standard curve during each assay, regardless of the format used" — [Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit user guide (MAN0011430)](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0011430_Pierce_BCA_Protein_Asy_UG.pdf); [Thermo Scientific Pierce Protein Assay Technical Handbook](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/brochures/1602063-Protein-Assay-Handbook.pdf); [Sigma BCA protocol](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/protocol/protein-biology/protein-quantitation/protein-determination-by-the-bicinchoninic-acid-bca-method); [Micro BCA user guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0011237_Micro_BCA_Protein_Asy_UG.pdf)
- Why it scores lower overall: the authority is a kit insert, not an international standard; the only real "required control condition" is the blank plus the standard curve, so the control-condition checker has little to do; and there is no QC-strain analogue giving an expected answer with a published tolerance.

**Alternative — PCR / qPCR reaction setup.** Standardization 4 (as *reporting*), checkability 3, robot compatibility 5, demo clarity 3, data availability 2.
- MIQE is a genuine, widely adopted community standard: "Minimum Information for Publication of Quantitative Real-Time PCR Experiments", Bustin et al. 2009, with "a checklist to accompany the initial submission of a manuscript", covering RNA quality, RT conditions, primer specificity, amplification efficiency and normalization — [MIQE 2009, Clinical Chemistry PDF](https://www.gene-quantification.de/miqe-bustin-et-al-clin-chem-2009.pdf); [MIQE précis, PMC2955025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2955025/); [MIQE (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIQE); recently revised as MIQE 2.0 — [MIQE 2.0 commentary, PMC12154065](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12154065/); [MIQE 2.0 record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391114866_MIQE_20_Revision_of_the_Minimum_Information_for_Publication_of_Quantitative_Real-Time_PCR_Experiments_Guidelines)
- Why it scores lower: MIQE prescribes *what you must report*, not numeric windows for temperature, primer concentration or cycle counts (those are assay- and enzyme-specific), so the static checker degrades into a completeness checklist rather than range arithmetic. Demo clarity suffers because "did it pass?" is less visually obvious than a plate of clear vs turbid wells.

**Alternative — Buffer / solution preparation from stock.** Standardization 2, checkability 5, robot compatibility 4, demo clarity 2, data availability 3. This is the easiest scope for unit and dilution arithmetic (C1V1 = C2V2, molarity ↔ mass, pH/pKa) and the hardest to make impressive: there is no published international standard defining a "correct" buffer recipe, and almost no required control conditions, so the "required control conditions" and "citation for every parameter" parts of the pitch have nothing to bite on. Useful as an internal unit-test suite for the checker, not as the headline scope. (No specific standard found; see Gaps.)

**Alternative — Colorimetric cell-viability assay (MTT/XTT).** Standardization 4, checkability 3, robot compatibility 4, demo clarity 3, data availability 2.
- There *is* an ISO standard with a hard numeric criterion: ISO 10993-5, "Biological evaluation of medical devices — Part 5: Tests for in vitro cytotoxicity", under which a material is considered non-cytotoxic if cell viability is ≥70% of the negative control — [measurlabs: cytotoxicity testing under ISO 10993-5](https://measurlabs.com/blog/cytotoxicity-testing-under-iso-10993-5/); [SenzaGen: ISO 10993-5 MTT/MEM elution test](https://senzagen.com/in-vitro-biocompatibility-testing/cytotoxicity-iso-10993-5-mtt-mem-elution-test/); [MD+DI compliance guide](https://www.mddionline.com/testing/cytotoxicity-testing-for-medical-devices-a-guide-to-compliance-best-practices); a third-party copy of the 2009 standard text circulates publicly — [ISO 10993-5:2009 copy](https://www.dent.chula.ac.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/file_1_0015.pdf) (unofficial; do not rely on it)
- Why it scores lower than MIC: the standard itself is paywalled and medical-device-framed, the biology (mammalian cell culture) is slower and less hackathon-demonstrable, and the 70% criterion is an *interpretation* threshold rather than a rich set of method parameters.

**Alternative — ELISA.** Standardization 2, checkability 3, robot compatibility 5, demo clarity 3, data availability 2. Robot support is excellent — Opentrons explicitly lists ELISA among Protocol Library workflows ([Intro to Protocol Library](https://opentrons.com/intro-to-protocol-library)) — but the method parameters are defined per kit by the manufacturer, not by an international standard, so "citation for every parameter" collapses to "citation = this vendor's insert". Weakest fit for the team's claim.

**Alternative — Enzyme kinetics (Michaelis–Menten).** Standardization 1, checkability 2, robot compatibility 3, demo clarity 2, data availability 2. The constraints that matter are about *fitting and experimental design* (substrate range spanning Km, initial-rate linearity, correct units on Vmax/Km) rather than published range tables; there is no analogue of the EUCAST QC table giving an expected answer with a tolerance for an ordinary lab reference system. (No governing standard found; see Gaps.)

### Inferences
- The discriminating feature across all these scopes is not whether a standard exists but whether the standard publishes **numeric values with tolerances plus mandatory control conditions plus an expected answer for a reference material**. Only broth microdilution has all three for free: 5×10^5 CFU/mL [2–8×10^5], 35±1 °C, 18±2 h, growth + sterility wells, and an expected MIC window for *E. coli* ATCC 25922. That triad is what makes a static checker look like a verifier rather than a linter.
- The team's motivating example (compare two approved antibiotics) *is* the textbook use of the primary scope, so there is no scope-fitting tax.
- The "it generalizes" claim is strongest if the architecture separates (i) a general unit/dilution/capacity engine from (ii) an assay-specific constraint pack loaded from citations. Demo the engine on one fully-populated AST pack, then show a deliberately thin second pack (BCA standard curve, using the Pierce 20–2000 µg/mL linear range and the mandatory per-assay standard curve) to evidence generalization without committing to a second full build.
- For the hardware/automation track, pair the AST pack with the Opentrons serial-dilution primitive and present the dry-run plus a set of mutation tests that the checker catches. That gives "testable results" without needing a wet lab.

### Gaps
- I found no governing published standard for buffer preparation or for enzyme-kinetics experimental design that would give numeric range constraints; the scoring for those two scopes rests on absence of evidence, which I am flagging rather than papering over.
- I did not locate an authoritative free source for Bradford-specific (as opposed to BCA-specific) linear ranges; the BCA numbers above are from Thermo/Sigma kit documentation, which is vendor literature, not a standard.
- ISO 10993-5 itself is paywalled; the ≥70% viability criterion is cited above from testing-laboratory and trade sources that restate it consistently, plus an unofficial copy of the standard text. Treat the number as well-attested but secondary-sourced.
- I did not find an ELISA-specific international standard analogous to ISO 20776-1, which is itself the finding for that scope.
